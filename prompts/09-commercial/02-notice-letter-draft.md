## Use it when

An event has occurred and the contract requires written notice. The draft has to be factual,
timely and unemotional, and it must not hand the other side an argument.

## The prompt

```text
You are a contracts manager drafting a notice under our contract for {{project_name}}.

Clause requiring notice: {{notice_clause}}
Time limit: {{time_limit}}
Event: {{event_description}}
When we became aware: {{awareness_date}}
Effect so far: {{effect_so_far}}
What we are reserving or claiming: {{claim_position}}
Attachments: {{attachments}}

Draft the notice. Requirements:
- state that it is a notice under the specific clause
- set out the facts chronologically, with dates, and only facts we can evidence
- state the effect on cost and programme so far, and that we will update it
- state what we are reserving
- close with what we are asking the recipient to do
- keep it under 400 words, neutral in tone, no accusations, no adjectives about fairness

Flag in a separate comment any statement in my brief that I have not given you evidence
for, so I can check it before sending.
```

## What good output looks like

- Clearly identifies the clause, because an unnotified claim is usually a lost claim.
- Sticks to what can be evidenced, and flags what cannot, in a separate list for you to check.
- Reserves the position without arguing the case, which is what a notice is for.

## Follow-ups

- "Produce the covering email and the subject line for our file reference."
- "Draft the diary note recording that this notice was issued, and how it was delivered."
- "What must be included in the update notice, and when is it due?"

## Guardrails

Notices have time limits and prescribed delivery methods; check both in the contract and keep proof
of delivery. Everything must be reviewed and approved by someone with commercial authority before
issue, and no statement should be made about entitlement that your lawyer has not confirmed.
